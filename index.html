    This application recomputes every quantity every 5 milliseconds (200 times per second). This document follows that order: force, then acceleration, then speed and position, then the braking decision.

    1. Basics

    Motion is computed in one dimension along the road axis. Every cycle all forces are summed, acceleration is computed, then speed and position are updated.

    Notation

    a always means acceleration or deceleration (m/s²), and m always means mass (kg or t). No symbol is used for two meanings.

    State variables

    Equations of motion

    Acceleration is one step inside the cycle, not the final result. It comes from the sum of forces divided by mass, which is the rate of change of momentum:

    p = m · v          dp/dt = ΣF          a = ΣF / m

    Terms summed at every step:

    a_fric     = applied × a_available
a_available= min( μ · g , a_brake )
a_regen    = regen × min(1, applied × 2.2)      # EV only
a_resist   = 0.045 + 0.00016 · v²               # rolling and aerodynamic
a_grade    = g × slope / 100
a_retard   = a_retardMax × avail(v)
a_net      = a_fric + a_regen + a_resist + a_retard − a_grade − a_throttle

v′ = max(0, v − a_net · dt)
s′ = s + ½ (v + v′) · dt

    Role of mass

    Deceleration is limited by two things of different nature:

    LimitFormulaEffect of mass
    Tyre–road frictionμ · gindependent of mass
    Brake capacityF_brake / minversely proportional to mass

    With a fixed brake force of 119.6 kN, deceleration is 4.60 m/s² at 26 t and 1.80 m/s² at 66.5 t. Mass rises 2.56 times, deceleration falls to 39 %. Deceleration can only stay equal if brake force rises in proportion to mass, for example through a load-sensing valve. That is why a_brake must come from a brake test on the unit, not from an assumption.

    2. The 200 Hz cycle

    The equations of motion are only valid if forces are treated as constant inside one time step. The simulator uses 5 milliseconds. The smaller the step, the smaller the error when forces change quickly.

    FrequencyStepComputed stopping distanceDifference
    200 Hz5 ms24.76 mreference
    20 Hz50 ms25.01 m+1.0 %
    4 Hz250 ms26.19 m+5.8 %

    If forces were truly constant, every frequency would give the same result. The difference appears when force changes inside one step. The 200 Hz figure is a numerical choice, not a description of the hardware: the radar delivers data every 20–50 ms and systems on the truck run at 20–50 Hz.

    3. Actuator delay

    A braking command does not act instantly. There is a fixed delay, then pressure rises gradually:

    latency = sensorDelay + processDelay + valveDelay     (default 0.23 s)
applied rises linearly by dt / brakeBuild              (default 0.85 s)
    StageTypicalHow to obtain it
    radar data age10–50 mstime between frames minus the time until use
    loop period5–20 msprogram cycle period
    compute time<1 mscomputation time per cycle
    relay and valve20–50 msuntil the solenoid is energised
    pressure to 75 %200–600 msUN R13 limit: 0.6 s

    Both values, latency and brakeBuild, are used inside the trigger formulas, not only in the actuator model. The simple formula v²/(2a) is not used because it gives a distance that is too short: at 40 km/h with a = 3 m/s² it yields 23.1 m, while the real distance is 27.8 m.

    Four sections follow, describing four alternative trigger rules. One unit runs one choice; the choice is made on the panel and there is no combination between choices. Each choice is complete with its own thresholds, braking levels, and limits.

    4. Logic 1 — relative TTC

    This logic judges danger from the approach speed at that moment.

    Approach speed comes from two sources, both available without extra computation: own speed from the vehicle sensor, and the object range-rate from Doppler. The radar does not measure object speed directly; it reports speed relative to the radar, therefore:

    v_lead  = v_ego + range-rate            (range-rate is negative when approaching)
closing = v_ego − v_lead = −range-rate

    This logic uses only the value of range-rate at that moment. It does not compute how that value changes over time, so no filter is needed. Computing that change is what logic 3 does, and that is where the 0.15 s filter is required.

    TTC   = gap / closing               (infinite when closing ≤ 0.05)
a_req = closing² / (2 · gap)
TTC_min = latency + v / (2 · a_available)

full_threshold = max(0.9 ; 1.1 · TTC_min)
soft_threshold = max(1.8 ; 1.5 · TTC_min)

TTC ≤ full_threshold or a_req ≥ 0.9 · a_available  → full brake
TTC ≤ soft_threshold or a_req ≥ 0.5 · a_available  → soft brake

    The thresholds are not fixed. They rise with TTC_min, the minimum time the truck needs to stop at the current speed and brake capability. Because a_available carries mass, a loaded unit uses earlier thresholds.

    SpeedLoaded 66.5 t (a 1.80)Empty 26 t (a 4.60)
    44 km/hfull 4.0 s · soft 5.4 sfull 1.7 s · soft 2.3 s
    60 km/h (loaded limit)full 5.4 s · soft 7.3 sfull 2.2 s · soft 3.1 s

    The weakness of this logic appears when two vehicles move at almost the same speed: closing approaches zero so TTC becomes very large, even though the gap is short. The trigger only acts after the leading vehicle brakes and an approach speed develops.

    5. Logic 2 — relative TTC plus safe distance

    Inside this logic there are two criteria: TTC as in logic 1, plus a time-based distance keeper. Both are part of one rule, not two logics running together.

    THW    = gap / v_ego
v_safe = gap / headway              (default headway 4 seconds)

keeper engages when THW ≤ headway; releases when THW > headway + 0.05

while engaged:
    a_need = (v − v_safe) / 2
    level  = 0.55 when a_need < 0.55 · a_available
             1.00 when a_need ≥ 0.55 · a_available

    The two criteria do not always coincide. The 4-second rule sets distance by time; a_req sets the deceleration needed to stop exactly at the object. They meet at one speed, v = 4 × a_available — about 26 km/h for a_available 1.80 m/s².

    Speed
    (object at rest)    		4-second distanceDistance when
    a_req = 50 % capacity    		Criterion
    arriving first
    30 km/h33 m39 ma_req
    50 km/h56 m107 ma_req

    Above 26 km/h, a_req reaches 50 % capacity before the gap shrinks to 4 seconds, so braking acts while the gap is still above 4 seconds. This is not a calculation error: 4 seconds guarantees reaction time against a moving vehicle, while a_req measures the ability to stop against a stationary object.

    6. Logic 3 — predictive

    This logic works from the change of speed of the object ahead, not from distance. The radar sends two values every cycle: range and approach speed (range-rate, measured through Doppler). From both the simulator computes how fast the approach speed is changing; that is the deceleration of the object ahead, a_lead.

    The reasoning is simple: when the truck ahead brakes, the approach speed grows. The radar sees that growth before the gap shrinks much, so the ego vehicle gets more time than with a distance-based logic.

    Computation steps

    1) a_lead   = filter_0.15s( d(−range_rate) / dt )
2) leadDist = distance the lead truck still needs to stop
3) budget   = gap + leadDist − margin(3 m)
4) a_ff     = requiredDecel(v_ego, budget, latency, build)

a_ff ≥ 0.55 · a_available → full brake
a_ff ≥ 0.25 · a_available → soft brake
    StepMeaningHow it is obtained
    1deceleration of the object aheaddifference of range-rate between cycles, smoothed over 0.15 s so radar noise does not trigger it
    2stopping distance of the object aheadv_lead² / (2 · a_lead); zero when the object has stopped, infinite when it moves without braking
    3road available to egocurrent gap plus the object stopping distance, minus a 3 m margin
    4deceleration requiredrequiredDecel, already accounting for latency and pressure build-up

    The core of step 3: what is computed is not the distance to the lead truck bumper, but the point where the lead truck will stop. That point is still moving while the lead truck has not stopped, and the radar estimates it from the deceleration in progress.

    Where a_lead comes from

    There are two different ways to obtain object speed, and they are not the same:

    MethodPrincipleDrawback
    Range differencerange measured twice, the difference divided by the time stepneeds a long step to stay quiet; a range resolution of 0.1–0.5 m makes the result coarse over 20–50 ms
    Doppler (used by this radar)the frequency shift of the echo is read directly at every measurement, without waiting for a long baselinegives only speed along the radar line, not lateral speed

    At 77 GHz the wavelength is 3.9 mm, so an approach speed of 1 m/s produces a 513 Hz shift. That shift is read every chirp, so speed is available almost every 20–50 ms — not the result of dividing ranges across a period of seconds.

    Object deceleration is obtained by differentiation: the change of speed divided by the time step.

    a_lead = (v₁ − v₂) / (t₂ − t₁)

example: (10.6 − 10.3) m/s ÷ (0.10 s) = 3.0 m/s²

    This is arithmetic over two radar readings, not an action on the vehicle: no brake is applied and ego speed is not changed by this step. Braking is decided only after the value is compared with a threshold.

    Because the difference of two close numbers is easily disturbed by noise, the result is smoothed with a first-order filter of 0.15 s. That is what the simulator computes every 5 millisecond cycle.

    One worked cycle

    ego 40 km/h (11.1 m/s) · lead 38 km/h (10.6 m/s) · gap 30 m

initial range-rate       = −0.50 m/s     (approaching slowly)
lead brakes at 3 m/s²  → range-rate falls 3 × 0.005 = 0.015 m/s per cycle
                         to −0.515 m/s
a_lead  = 0.015 / 0.005 = 3.0 m/s²       (after the 0.15 s filter)

leadDist = 10.6² / (2 × 3) = 18.7 m
budget   = 30 + 18.7 − 3 = 45.7 m
a_ff     = requiredDecel(11.1 ; 45.7 ; 0.23 ; 0.85) = 1.60 m/s²

1.60 ≥ 0.55 × 1.80 (= 0.99)  →  full brake

    At a 30 m gap and a speed difference of only 0.5 m/s, a TTC-based logic sees no danger at all. The predictive logic already commands full brake, because the truck ahead is slowing faster than ego.

    Object at rest: the TTC criterion inside this logic

    This logic stands alone. Inside it two parts run every cycle: feed-forward from a_lead, and the TTC + a_req criteria. The TTC part is not taken from another logic; it is part of this one.

    Feed-forward is used only for objects that have moved (leadMovingSeen: speed above 1 m/s once is enough). The reason is that the radar gives no object identity: a stopped truck, a sign, a fence, and an embankment return reflections that cannot be distinguished. Objects at rest are handled by the TTC + a_req criteria, which are also inside this logic.

    Object at rest, 60 km/hLogic 3 — predictiveLogic 1 — relative TTC
    gap 67 mcollision 29.2 km/hcollision 29.2 km/h
    gap 120 mstops, 21.9 m leftstops, 21.9 m left
    gap 170 mstops, 31.5 m leftstops, 31.5 m left
    gap 250 mstops, 67.1 m leftstops, 67.1 m left

    The results are identical because the TTC part is the same. It means that for objects at rest from the start, logic 3 is as capable as logic 1; its advantage appears only when the object has moved.

    If feed-forward were forced onto a stationary object, a_lead would be zero, so the formula degrades into a stopping-distance criterion and triggers at absurd range: at 60 km/h soft brake would engage with the object still 319 m ahead (19 s), full brake at 151 m (9 s). That is why the part is restricted.

    A limit remains: if an object stops before the radar ever saw it move (a truck already stopped around a bend), feed-forward does not work and this logic is only as strong as its TTC criterion — including failing to avoid a collision at 67 m at 60 km/h.

    7. Logic 4 — pure distance keeper

    This logic uses only gap and own speed. The speed of the object ahead is not used, so TTC is not computed and there is no a_req.

    THW    = gap / v_ego
v_safe = gap / headway              (default headway 4 seconds)

keeper engages when THW ≤ headway; releases when THW > headway + 0.05

while engaged:
    a_need = (v − v_safe) / 2
    level  = 0.55 when a_need < 0.55 · a_available
             1.00 when a_need ≥ 0.55 · a_available

    The rule follows speed: at 40 km/h the limit is 44 m, at 50 km/h 56 m, and at 60 km/h (the loaded-unit limit) 67 m. Changing the headway on the panel shifts the safe distance automatically.

    What must be understood: this logic does not recognise danger that approaches quickly. Against a stationary object at an 8-second gap (120 m), it stays passive until the gap shrinks to the limit, and at that point the stopping distance is already longer than the remaining gap.

    Condition (66.5 t, 60 km/h)Logic 2Logic 4
    Object at rest, 8-second gap (120 m)brakes at 0.28 s, stops with 21.9 m leftbrakes at 3.53 s, collision 29.3 km/h
    Object at rest, exactly 4-second gap (67 m)collision 29.2 km/hcollision 29.7 km/h

    The second row underlines the limit of the rule, not a difference between the two logics: at 60 km/h the vehicle only stops after travelling 98.1 m (measured from the first row: 120 m minus 21.9 m left), so 67 m is not enough for any choice.

    The difference between them lies in their contents: logic 2 contains TTC and the safety override that can raise the command to full brake, while logic 4 contains only the two-level distance keeper. When object speed is nearly equal, both behave identically because TTC becomes infinite.

    That is why logic 4 is used to enforce the distance rule, not as a replacement for the AEB function.

    8. Safety override

    This criterion is part of logic 1, 2, and 3. It acts every cycle without waiting for the TTC threshold:

    leadDist = object at rest ? 0 : v_lead² / (2 · a_lead)
budget   = gap + leadDist − margin
demand   = requiredDecel(v, budget, latency, build)

demand ≥ 0.85 · a_available → full brake
demand ≥ 0.40 · a_available → soft brake

    Its purpose is single: the brake must not wait until the required deceleration exceeds the capability of the truck. Because a_available is derived from mass, this criterion acts earlier on a loaded unit. Logic 4 does not contain it, because its content is only the distance keeper.

    9. Braking levels

    The system uses two levels, matching the unit installed:

    TermLevelEmpty 26 tLoaded 66.5 t
    soft brake0.552.53 m/s²0.99 m/s²
    full brake1.004.60 m/s²1.80 m/s²

    Level is a fraction of capacity, not an absolute figure. Because capacity differs, the absolute values differ even at the same percentage.

    10. Stopping distance and minimum TTC

    d_stop = v·T − a·T²/6 + (v − a·T/2)² / (2a) + v·latency
TTC_min = latency + v / (2a)

    TTC_min is the smallest TTC that still allows the vehicle to stop. It grows with speed, so a TTC threshold given as a constant cannot hold across the whole speed range.

    15. Model limits

    • One dimension: no evasive manoeuvre and no wheel rotation.
    • Load transfer and rotational inertia are not modelled.
    • ABS is not modelled; deceleration is assumed to be reached without lock cycles.
    • Brake fade from heat is not modelled; a_brake is treated as constant.
    • Friction coefficient is assumed uniform along the route.
    • Latency, brakeBuild, and a_brake are still assumptions until unit measurements exist.
    • Speed is computed from displacement, not from wheel rotation.
